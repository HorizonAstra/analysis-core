## Reading results in this domain

Each capability says how to read its own output, and that is where the specific
caveats live. What follows holds across them.

**A group label on a sample came from a person, not from the measurement.**
Nothing here predicts outcome or class. Where a study carries no such label, no
outcome association is possible; a label that appears to be encoded in a sample's
name is an inference to confirm before acting on, not a column.

**Recurring across samples is the default rather than a finding.** Lists of
features that differ somewhere in a tissue run long, and a large share of them
recur in most samples. Counting recurrence over whole lists separates nothing.
Restrict each sample to its strongest before counting, and say that you did.

**Watch what sits at the top of a ranking.** Families of genes that track tissue
quality and cell state rather than biology, mitochondrial and ribosomal genes
among them, surface at the top of these rankings routinely. Say when they are
there instead of building an explanation on them.

**Feature identifiers are often accessions rather than names.** Map them before
showing them to anyone, and where a study carries nothing to map with, say so
rather than guessing at a name.

**Settings vary from sample to sample.** What each sample was run with, and how
well that fit, is recorded per sample. Read it before comparing samples to each
other, and surface one that fits much worse than the rest rather than pooling it
in silently.

**Results that already existed were not necessarily produced here.** Where a
result carries no record of the code that made it, it can be used and shown but
not reproduced from here, and it should be described that way.
