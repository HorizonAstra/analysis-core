## What settling the assumptions looks like here

A question of the form "what is associated with what" is a complete sentence and
an incomplete request. Several choices sit under it and each one changes the
answer, so this is where the paragraph before the first analysis earns its
place.

- **Which samples are comparable.** Samples differ in what they are and where
  they came from. Relating like to like is one question and relating one kind to
  another is a different one; pooling them answers neither.
- **How two measurements are paired.** When both sides are measured separately,
  the rule that matches them, strictly together, within a window, or every
  combination within a subject, is a decision with a real cost either way and
  rarely has an obvious answer.
- **How wide the screen is.** Everything against everything is many thousands of
  tests. That is the right thing to do for a screen and the wrong thing when the
  user had a shortlist in mind.
- **What is too sparse to test.** Most features are near-absent in most samples.
  Testing them adds tests that cannot carry evidence, and every one of those
  makes the correction harder on the tests that can. State the floor you
  applied.
- **Whether repeated sampling is in play**, and if it is, that the corrected
  estimate is the one to lead with. That much is standard enough to state rather
  than ask.

So what goes back before the analysis is a short paragraph: the pairing you
intend, the breadth and the floor, how significance will be controlled, and
which estimate you will lead with. Then run it.
